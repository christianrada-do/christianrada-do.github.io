Kawasaki Disease

By Dr. Christian Rada, DO · Clinician tool

Consider Kawasaki disease with ≥ 3 days of fever and any principal feature, or an infant ≤ 6 months with ≥ 7 days of unexplained fever. Based on the CHOP Kawasaki Disease pathway (ED and inpatient, adapted from the 2017 AHA guideline). For possible MIS-C, KD with shock or macrophage activation syndrome, see the notes below. Initial treatment

Response and subsequent treatment

Responsive: afebrile (< 38.0°C) and improving about 48 hours after IVIG (or adjunctive treatment). Resistant: fever > 38.0°C or lingering symptoms more than 24–36 hours after IVIG finishes. Fever in the first 24 hours after IVIG is typical and does not prompt retreatment.

Discharge and follow-up

  • Low-dose aspirin until cardiology says to stop (longer with coronary abnormalities). Avoid ibuprofen and other NSAIDs while on aspirin.
  • If steroids were given: finish the 2–3 week oral taper.
  • PCP within 2–3 days. Cardiology with a repeat echo in about 2 weeks (sooner with significant coronary findings). Rheumatology in 1–2 weeks if steroids were given. Book appointments before discharge if possible.
  • Check the temperature daily for 1–2 weeks; return to the ED for > 38°C or returning KD signs before the cardiology or rheumatology visit.
  • No live vaccines for 11 months after IVIG. In flu season, give the (inactivated) flu shot before discharge if not already given.

Pitfalls and special situations

Consider KD in: infants under 6 months with prolonged fever and irritability; prolonged fever with unexplained aseptic meningitis; prolonged fever with unexplained or culture-negative shock, cervical lymphadenitis or retro/parapharyngeal phlegmon not responding to antibiotics. A documented viral or bacterial infection (e.g., strep) can coexist with KD.
KD with shock or macrophage activation syndrome: consult rheumatology and cardiology, give IVIG and additional treatments promptly, and consider ICU consult or management.
MIS-C: review the MIS-C pathway; consult as needed about further labs and imaging.
After IVIG: don't recheck the ESR (IVIG raises it). After 2 or more IVIG doses, check for hemolysis (CBC, reticulocytes, LDH, LFTs) 12–24 hours later. Discuss with immunology first if IgA deficient.
