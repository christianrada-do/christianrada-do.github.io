Febrile Infant ≤ 56 Days (CHOP)

By Dr. Christian Rada, DO · Clinician tool

Well-appearing infants ≤ 56 days with community-onset fever (rectal temperature ≥ 38.0°C / 100.4°F in the past 24 hours). Based on the CHOP Febrile Infant clinical pathway (ED and inpatient, adapted from the AAP 2021 guideline). Nothing entered here is saved or sent. AAP guideline version

Infant

Appearance and history

Signs of an ill-appearing infant

Tachypnea, apnea or grunting; pale, gray, cyanotic or mottled color, poor perfusion; poor tone, limp, weak cry; bulging fontanelle; irritability, inconsolability, decreased alertness, listlessness or lethargy.

HSV risk factors

Maternal vesicles and/or fever within 48 hours before or after delivery; postnatal oral HSV exposure; vesicles or mucous membrane ulcers; abnormal mental status or seizure; hypothermia; CSF pleocytosis or RBCs; leukopenia; thrombocytopenia.

After admission

Discharge criteria (negative cultures)

Well-appearing and feeding well; blood, urine and CSF cultures (if obtained) negative for at least 24–36 hours, per local lab reporting (confirm with the lab if the record isn't updated); HSV studies negative if sent; PCP notified with follow-up within 72 hours.

Low-risk 22–28 days: admit and observe. If the baby becomes ill-appearing or the blood culture turns positive, do an LP and start antibiotics. If only the urine culture turns positive with a negative UA and the baby is well, an LP can be deferred and empiric antibiotics started. Inflammatory markers can be normal early: if still febrile and first seen less than 6 hours after fever onset, consider repeating them.

Any positive culture or HSV PCR

HSV PCR positive: consult ID. Positive cultures: target antibiotics to speciation and sensitivities; repeat the blood culture (before changing antibiotics) for bacteremia; ID consult and meningitic dosing for CSF growth; renal and bladder ultrasound (consider urology) for UTI. If no LP was done, perform one promptly (before antibiotics if not started). After failed attempts, consider point-of-care ultrasound for hematoma or referral to IR.

LP indicated but no CSF obtained

Inpatient LP is preferred for most. Weigh age, inflammatory markers (22–56 days) and initial appearance; use shared decision-making; consider ID consultation.

Other tests

CSF: Gram stain and culture, protein, glucose, cell count and differential, and save a specimen. CSF enterovirus PCR if pleocytosis (consider blood enterovirus PCR too). Stool bacterial panel or culture if mucus or blood in the stool; consider a GI virus panel. Chest X-ray as indicated. ANC under 1,000 should be interpreted in clinical context.

Based on the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia (CHOP) Febrile Infant Clinical Pathway, Emergency Department and Inpatient (adapted from the AAP 2021 guideline, Pantell RH et al., Pediatrics 2021; last revised August 2026), summarized in our own words.
