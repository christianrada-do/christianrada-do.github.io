Developmental Milestones

By Dr. Christian Rada, DO · For families and clinicians

CDC developmental milestones: things most children (75% or more) can do by each age. For developmental surveillance; not a substitute for standardized, validated developmental screening tools. Nothing entered here is saved or sent.

CDC's advice to families: “If your child is not meeting one or more milestones, has lost skills he or she once had, or you have other concerns, act early. Talk with your child's doctor, share your concerns, and ask about developmental screening.” Early intervention programs: cdc.gov/FindEI.

Get checked soon if…

Your child loses skills they once had (talking, walking, gestures or social skills), at any age. This needs a prompt check; some children need a neurology or genetics review.
You or your child's clinician are worried about development. Worry alone is a reason to screen.
  • Doesn't respond to sounds, or any worry about hearing or speech: ask for a hearing test.
  • Doesn't smile or engage with people, or has little back-and-forth interaction.
  • Autism signs: not responding to their name by 12 months, not pointing to show you things by about 14–18 months, no pretend play by 18 months, poor eye contact.
  • Movement: not sitting by 9 months, not walking by 18 months, a strong hand preference before 18 months, stiffness or floppiness, or using one side of the body more than the other.
  • Talking: no babbling with consonant sounds (like “ba” or “da”) by 9 months, fewer than about 6 words by 18 months, no two-word phrases by 24 months, or speech still hard to understand after age 2.

Babies born before 37 weeks: judge the age-based signs by corrected age until 24 months corrected age. Losing skills and being worried count at any age. Correction can make some babies look further along than they are, especially for talking, social and movement skills, so don't wait if you're worried.

Also screen at any visit when there's a concern. The autism screen (M-CHAT-R/F) is for ages 16–30 months (corrected age for babies born early).

If a milestone is missed

These steps happen at the same time. No diagnosis is needed to start.

  1. Do a standardized developmental screen now (for example ASQ-3, PEDS or SWYC; add the M-CHAT-R/F if autism is a concern).
  3. Get a hearing test for any delay in talking or any worry about hearing, even if the newborn hearing screen was passed.
  4. Depending on the pattern, the clinician may refer to developmental-behavioral pediatrics, neurology or genetics; promptly if skills have been lost.

Clinicians: refer within 7 days of identifying a concern. Early intervention vs school district follows the child's actual (chronological) age, not corrected age.
