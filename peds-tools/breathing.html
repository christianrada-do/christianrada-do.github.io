Box Breathing

By Dr. Christian Rada, DO · For kids, teens and parents

Breathe with the square

Feeling panicky or really worried? Slow breathing may help your body feel calmer. Pick a pattern, press Start and follow along. Breathe gently; there's no wrong way to do this.

How to do it

The square (older kids, teens and parents)

  1. Breathe in through your nose, counting to 4 (or 3)
  2. Hold, counting to 4 (or 3)
  3. Breathe out through your mouth, counting to 4 (or 3)
  4. Hold, counting to 4 (or 3), then start again

Counting to 3 is easier and works well for most kids. Counting to 4 is the classic square for teens and adults.

Younger kids (easier, no holding)

Breathe in like you're smelling a flower, counting to 3. Breathe out like you're blowing a bubble, counting to 3. A grown-up can do it with them.

Don't like holding your breath?

Breathe in for 4 and out for 6, with no holding. A longer breath out helps you relax.

How long?

Start with about 1 minute. Keep going up to about 5 minutes if it's helping, and stop whenever you feel calmer.

Keep it gentle

  • Breathe softly and normally. Don't force big, deep breaths.
  • Feeling a little lightheaded or dizzy is common. Stop, breathe normally and rest.
  • Skip the holds if you have asthma, a heart condition, seizures (epilepsy), or are pregnant. Slow breathing is still fine: use "No holding: in 4, out 6".
  • Stop and rest if you feel dizzy or faint, tingling in your hands or around your mouth, chest pain or tightness, more short of breath, a racing or pounding heart, a headache or feel sick, or more panicky.

Other ways to calm down

5-4-3-2-1

Slowly name 5 things you can see, 4 things you can touch, 3 things you can hear, 2 things you can smell and 1 thing you can taste. It helps your mind come back to right now. For little kids: "Find 5 blue things in the room."

Squeeze and let go

Squeeze your fists tight like you're holding a lemon, hold for a few seconds, then let go and feel them relax. Do the same with your shoulders, then your legs.

If worry or panic keeps coming back, tell a trusted adult and your child's doctor. These are calming skills, not medical care, and they don't replace talking with a doctor, a counselor or a crisis line.

Get help now

Call 911 if any of these happen. Breathing exercises are for worry and panic feelings, not for these:

  • Chest pain during or after exercise, or pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back
  • Fainting or passing out (not just feeling dizzy)
  • Trouble breathing that isn't getting better, blue lips or fingertips, or wheezing or noisy breathing
  • Slurred speech, confusion or trouble moving
  • Swelling of the face or throat, or hives
  • They may have taken pills or something harmful
  • Chest pain, a racing heart or feeling faint in a child with a heart condition or a family history of sudden heart death
  • Anyone is in danger

Thinking about hurting yourself or ending your life? You are not alone, and help is there right now, day or night:

Call 911 Call 988 Text 988 Text HOME to 741741

988 is the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Call 911 if someone has already been hurt or is in immediate danger.
